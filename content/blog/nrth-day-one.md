I went to the opening night of Elevate at Meridian Hall in Toronto on Tuesday night. About an hour and fifteen minutes in, a video played that ended with "The world has changed, and so we're changing too." Then the CEO came out on stage as the CEO of **Nrth**.

Elevate renamed itself in the middle of its own kickoff.

Looking back, the clues were there. The branding in the hall felt a little off, and our badges had no name or logo on them. She called that out on stage: you could leave your badge blank to prove you were there for the reveal, or grab a Nrth sticker on the way out. When I walked out of the hall, the whole place had changed over. New signs, and blue everywhere. It was a fun thing to see happen live.

## Why the rename

The CEO said that when they started Elevate in 2017, the idea was that you shouldn't have to leave Canada to build a company that matters globally. They still believe that. But she said people don't only want a spotlight anymore. They want direction: which technologies actually matter, who to partner with, and how to make decisions when things move this fast. That's what Nrth is supposed to be.

## The rest of the night

Before the reveal there was a round of short startup pitches. They ranged from at-home breast cancer screening to software for grocery stores that still order stock the way they did 30 years ago.

After it, the host summed up the program pretty honestly: "AI, a little more AI, a little more AI, a little more AI, and then basketball." A few things stuck with me:

- **Cohere and TD** talked about AI moving past pilots and proofs of concept. Their point was that the hard part now isn't the technology. It's getting a company to actually adopt it.
- **DuckDuckGo** argued that the best way to protect people's data is to not collect it in the first place. They also showed a spoof ad for "anti-surveillance smart glasses", which were just normal sunglasses.
- **Mistral** talked about sovereign AI: owning your models, data and compute so nobody can switch them off on you.

As someone who builds AI products, the adoption point is the one I keep thinking about. Nobody on stage was short on models. Everyone was trying to figure out how to get people to use them well.
